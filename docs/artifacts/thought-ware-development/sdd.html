Simulation-Driven Development, in Depth

Personas as deterministic app states, friction profiles as the real input, hallucination as a permissible upstream feature, and persona priority as a Product Owner call that moves over time.

Candidate for global promotion - pilot in progress

A persona is a state, not a character

The tempting shortcut is asking the model to roleplay a user - "pretend you're a beginner and tell me what confuses you." That produces a plausible-sounding opinion, not a checkable fact, and it's a different opinion every time you ask. SDD (the running skill calls it USDD - User-Simulation-Driven Development) rejects that shortcut entirely: a persona here is music.guidanceLevel.v1 plus an answered-ask notable, seeded into localStorage before the scenario runner taps a single button. The runner then drives the same taps a real user of that level would make and asserts what that user must - or must not - see.

Persona levelSeeded stateWhat diverges (notables)
Beginnerlevel=beginner, ask answeredfirstrun/tunefirst/savebasics tips; no whynote or scaletip theory prose
Intermediatelevel=intermediate, ask answeredwhynote, composeintro, transposetip
Advancedlevel=advanced, ask answeredwhynote, scaletip

Same taps, seeded state swapped, opposite assertions - both provably green. That's the whole trick: "run any app user on any task" becomes a replayable claim instead of an improvised performance, because the persona is data the runner seeds, not a character the model stays in.

Friction profiles are the real input

A friction profile is not a backlog someone invented at a whiteboard. It's built the same way every time: take a persona, list what actually went wrong for them in a real session, and cite the evidence - an operator quote, a scenario screenshot, a code trace - next to each one. Anything without evidence doesn't count as friction yet, it's a guess.

Composing the right amount of hallucination

The model is free to hallucinate candidate features, candidate fixes, candidate copy - as many as it wants, as fast as it wants. That's not a risk to manage, it's the cheap half of the loop working as intended. What makes it safe is where the hallucination is allowed to live: upstream of the gate, generating guesses, never inside the gate, deciding whether a guess counts.

"Tight loop" doesn't mean the model is trusted more each round - it means every round produces a bounded, checkable outcome regardless of how the candidate was generated. A hallucinated guess that's wrong fails the persona scenario for a specific, readable reason; a hallucinated guess that's right passes the exact same gate a careful guess would have to clear. The loop can't be gamed by generating more confidently, only by generating something that actually survives the scenario.

Persona priority is a Product Owner call, and it moves

The wiki records what each persona needs; it doesn't decide which persona gets built for first. That's a product call, made by the Product Owner, re-scored as the product matures - not a fixed ranking baked into the spec forever.

Concretely, on this app: the theory-literate personas came first. The Conservatory Pianist is described in the persona registry itself as "the most credibility-sensitive persona" - one wrong enharmonic spelling and that user is gone for good, no second chance. That's an asymmetric cost: getting theory wrong for an expert is catastrophic and permanent, while a rough edge in beginner onboarding is just a rough edge, fixable next sprint. So the credibility bar had to be earned first, deterministically, before beginner experience was worth investing in at all - a beginner win nobody trusts because the theory underneath is shaky isn't a win.

Once the deterministic theory canon was audited and green (see the theory-engine verification work the app already cites elsewhere), the highest-scored goalpost shifted: a persona-conditional beginner orientation scored 80 on Impact × Reach × Ease - the top item in the queue - precisely because it no longer risked the credibility bar the first phase had already secured. That's what "the priority moves" means in practice: not a mood change, a re-score triggered by what got proven in the phase before it.

The wiki doesn't set the order; it records what's true once the order picks a persona. Priority is the Product Owner's call to make and remake - the friction profile and the persona registry are what that call gets checked against, not what makes it.
