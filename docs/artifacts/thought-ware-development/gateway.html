User-Centric Development

A method for steering agentic AI development with a wiki that makes business rules deterministic - so an app built mostly by agents stays verifiable, not just plausible.

Start here ↓ Read the full method
Who does which job

HiTL is not technical, and not a subject-matter expert.

A problem gets reported as a spoken phrase or fragment - speech-to-text, not an implementation direction. "the studio thing is confusing when I'm playing" is a valid, sufficient report. Nobody on the human side is expected to say which component, which state, or which fix.

The agents are the CTO and the subject-matter experts. A coach suite - UX, accessibility, music theory - turns that fragment into a wiki entry written to whatever level of detail the TDD/SDD/UAT loops actually need to run. The wiki is the human-computer-interaction layer's spec, written by the SMEs, for a HiTL who never has to speak their language.

The claim

Hallucination is upstream of the gate, never inside it.

The model is free to generate - guess at features, hallucinate task ideas, propose whatever it wants. Nothing it generates ships until it survives a deterministic loop that doesn't care what the model believes.

The human half of the loop

Deterministic doesn't mean unattended. It means the human's judgment is verifiable too.

A PR opens a real, branch-tagged preview - a githack link at that exact commit, live before anything merges - and the operator gets it on their phone. From there it's a guided tour, not a report: server logs tail in the background while the operator taps through, and every input is narrated back play-by-play, step by step, as it lands - not "run this and tell me what you see," the agent watches the same evidence arrive in real time.

PR opens → Branch-tagged preview URL, live → Logs tail; play-by-play narration → Fails + screenshots, accepted as feedback → Next revision, same PR

Screenshots and voice notes are the feedback, not a bug report written after the fact - the loop closes on the same evidence both sides just watched happen.

What the operator actually does, per cycle

Tens of iterations. Hundreds, sometimes. One number typed, each time.

Dev loops and UAT loops don't run once - they run 10s to 100s of times across a working session. The loop still has to stop at a real breakpoint and ask a human something, but the ask is a single numbered option: proceed to the next goalpost, or don't. Minimal typing, by design - the human-factors cost of staying in the loop has to stay near zero, or nobody stays in it for iteration 40.

It keeps stopping and asking, one number at a time, until there is no goalpost left to advance to. Then the loop changes shape entirely - see below.

Composing the right amount

Too little generation and nothing new gets tried. Too much and nothing converges.

Agentic engineering is composing the right amount of probabilistic hallucination - of app, of personas, of scenarios - against a Playwright scenario harness that scores each attempt. The generation stays loose; the scoring stays exact. Iteration is what happens in between.

Every candidate feature is a guess about what a persona needs next. Most guesses are wrong in some detail. That's fine - a wrong guess against a real scenario fails for a specific, readable reason, and the next guess is better informed than the last one. The harness is what makes "better informed" a fact, not a feeling.

The metaphor, exact

It's like a harmonic getting in tune.

You don't leap to the right pitch. You turn the peg, check the distance, turn again - each pass smaller than the last, until the tuner reads zero.

That's the loop. A hallucinated candidate is a turn of the peg - imprecise on its own, but checkable against something exact. The persona scenario is the tuner: it doesn't hand you the right answer, it tells you how far off you are and in which direction. Distance shrinks every pass. That's what "iterate" is supposed to mean and usually doesn't.

What gets built next

Prioritized by persona priority, not by backlog position.

A feature earns its place in the sprint by which persona's dismissal trigger it closes - not by who asked loudest or what's next on a list. A fix that protects the Conservatory Pianist from one wrong enharmonic spelling outranks a dozen conveniences for a persona who was never going to leave over them.

Why any of this can be checked at all

A business-logic wiki, kept as the single source of truth, is what makes the whole app verifiable.

A scenario can only fail "for a real reason" if there's a written, deterministic statement of what correct means - a wiki the agents read before they write code, not tribal knowledge in one person's head. Without it, "looks right" is the only bar anyone can check against, and that bar moves depending on who's looking. With it, a persona's assertion traces back to a rule someone actually wrote down, and disagreements are a diff, not an argument.

The wiki is the shared abstraction, not a document for one side of the loop. The human reads it to know what "correct" was supposed to mean; the agent reads the same page to know what to build and what to check. Same source, both readers - which is exactly what makes the loop deterministic: neither side is verifying the other against a private understanding, both are verifying against the one thing that's actually written down.

The wiki-as-institutional-memory idea traces to Andrej Karpathy's llm-wiki gist - not reinvented here, just applied to business rules instead of general knowledge.

When there's no goalpost left

A different vendor's model gets to refuse every claim that isn't proven.

Once every goalpost passes, the loop's shape changes: PR, then CI, then an adversarial volley between two different vendors' models, not one model checking its own work. The highest-thinking Claude tier writes; a skeptical Codex pass reviews - and denies any claim that isn't backed by deterministic, TDD-proven evidence. "It should work" and "it looks right" are not evidence. A passing scenario is.

Only after that survives does a GitHub App bot identity file the formal approve - a separate identity from the one that authored the PR, because a model cannot credibly approve its own work under its own name. Merge, then back into the TDD/UAT loop for the next cycle. The volley is a gate on the way out, not a courtesy step.

The proof case

A song about friction, written by the loop testing itself.

The pilot's sharpest artifact wasn't a bug fix - it was using the app's own music theory engine to co-write a real song about compound engineering, grounded in the engine's own output before a single lyric was written:

Circle.scaleInKey('F','mixolydian') -> F G A Bb C D Eb
Circle.diatonicInKey('F','mixolydian') -> ... Dm(vi) Eb(bVII)

Dm is the vi of F Mixolydian - one coherent palette, verified by the theory engine before the words existed. The song is about the loop. The loop wrote the song's harmony.
