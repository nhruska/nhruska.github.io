The Method, in Full

Steering agentic AI development with a wiki that makes business rules deterministic - proven on a real app, against real users, with a music theory engine as the judge.

Candidate for global promotion - pilot in progress

Who does which job

The human-in-the-loop is not technical, and not a subject-matter expert. A problem gets reported as a spoken phrase or fragment - speech-to-text, not an implementation direction. "the studio thing is confusing when I'm playing" is a valid, sufficient report; nobody on the human side has to say which component, which state, or which fix.

The agents are the CTO and the subject-matter experts. This app runs a coach suite - UX, accessibility, music theory - whose job is turning that fragment into a wiki entry written to whatever level of detail the TDD/SDD/UAT loops actually need to run live. The wiki is the human-computer-interaction layer's spec, authored by the SMEs, for a HiTL who never has to speak their language.

The claim

Most "AI-assisted development" trusts the model's judgment on whether something works. This doesn't. The model is free to generate - guess at features, hallucinate task ideas, propose whatever it wants - because nothing it generates ships until it survives a deterministic loop that doesn't care what the model believes.

Hallucination is upstream of the gate, never inside it. An LLM proposing a candidate feature or task is fine - even useful, since it's cheap to generate many. What makes this safe is that every candidate has to become a persona-run scenario before it counts for anything, and that scenario either fails for a real reason or it doesn't exist yet as a goalpost worth building toward.

The loop - red, green, regression

Friction profile (real user, real observed problem) → Goalpost -> persona scenario → Run it - must FAIL (red) → Build until it passes (green) → Keep it as a regression, forever

A scenario that passes before the build exists means the goalpost was already met, or the assertion is too weak - the fix is a sharper assertion, never a lowered bar. This is TDD with one substitution: the unit under test is a person completing a use case, not a function returning a value.

The UAT loop - the human half, made just as verifiable

A PR triggers .github/workflows/pr-preview.yml on opened/synchronize/reopened - before any merge, not after. It posts a branch-tagged raw.githack.com preview URL as a PR comment: the exact commit, served live, over https (so the in-app mic-based tuner works on mobile too). The commit-pinned form is for isolated per-commit testing only - it 404s once the branch is deleted - so the branch-form URL is the one that tracks the latest push and is the one actually handed to the operator.

From there it is a guided tour, not a report. Server logs tail in the background before the first tap; each input is narrated back play-by-play, step by step, as it lands. The operator is never asked "run this and tell me what you see" - the agent watches the same evidence arrive in real time, on the same timeline. Fails and screenshots are accepted as the feedback itself, not transcribed into a bug report after the fact, and the next revision lands on the same PR. Deterministic on the machine side; verifiable, not just reported, on the human side too.

What the operator actually does, per cycle

Dev loops and UAT loops don't run once - they run 10s to 100s of times across a working session. The loop still has to stop at a real breakpoint and ask a human something, but the ask is a single numbered option: proceed to the next goalpost, or don't. Minimal typing is not a nicety here - the human-factors cost of staying in the loop has to stay near zero, or nobody stays in it past the first dozen iterations. It keeps stopping and asking, one number at a time, until there is no goalpost left to advance to - at which point the loop changes shape entirely (see below).

Personas are app states, not narratives

A persona here is music.guidanceLevel.v1 plus an answered-ask notable, seeded before the script runs - not a description an LLM stays in character for. That's what makes "run any app user on any task" a real, replayable claim instead of a roleplay: the same persona against the same flow produces the same assertions every time.

Why any of this can be checked at all

A scenario can only fail "for a real reason" if there's a written, deterministic statement of what correct means - a wiki, not tribal knowledge in one person's head. Without it, "looks right" is the only bar anyone can check against, and it moves depending on who's looking.

The wiki is the shared abstraction, not a document for one side of the loop. The human reads it to know what "correct" was supposed to mean; the agent reads the same page to know what to build and what to check. Same source, both readers - that symmetry is what makes the loop deterministic. Neither side is verifying the other against a private understanding; both are verifying against the one thing actually written down. The wiki-as-institutional-memory idea traces to Andrej Karpathy's llm-wiki gist - applied here to business rules instead of general knowledge, not reinvented.

When there's no goalpost left

Once every goalpost passes, the loop's shape changes: PR, then CI, then an adversarial volley between two different vendors' models, not one model checking its own work. The highest-thinking Claude tier writes; a skeptical Codex pass reviews - and denies any claim that isn't backed by deterministic, TDD-proven evidence. "It should work" and "it looks right" are not evidence. A passing scenario is.

Only after that survives does a GitHub App bot identity file the formal approve - a separate identity from the one that authored the PR, because a model cannot credibly approve its own work under its own name. Merge, then back into the TDD/UAT loop for the next cycle. The volley is a gate on the way out, not a courtesy step.

The proof case: a song about friction, written by the loop testing itself

The pilot's sharpest artifact wasn't a bug fix - it was using the app's own music theory engine to co-write a real song about compound engineering, working through problems and naming weak points. Grounded in the engine's own output, not vibes:

Circle.scaleInKey('F', 'mixolydian')     -> F G A Bb C D Eb
Circle.diatonicInKey('F', 'mixolydian')  -> F(I) Gm(ii) Adim(iii) Bb(IV) Cm(v) Dm(vi) Eb(bVII)

Dm is the vi of F Mixolydian - one coherent palette, verified by the theory engine before a single lyric was written. The verse leans the minor's shade; the chorus opens the Mixolydian door. The song is about the loop. The loop wrote the song's harmony.

Status, honestly

This is a pilot on one real app (the music app hosted here), not yet a portable methodology. The USDD skill that runs this loop already says so itself: "candidate for global promotion after the music-app pilot." A related discipline - simulation-driven development on real deterministic tests, not just personas - has already crossed over into at least one other project's engineering rules. That crossover is the actual signal this is more than a one-app trick, not a claim made here in advance of it.

Put plainly: this is what "user-centric development" means when it's a method and not a slogan - the human at the center reports in their own fragments, never learns the tooling's language, and the system is what bends to meet them. That's the same claim Problem Solutions' own stated mission makes - "competitive advantages through human-centered emerging technology" - built here first, at personal scale, before it ever has to be argued for at company scale.
